Watching a big game with a group is great. Watching it alone is
underrated, and I want to make the case for it.

When it's just you, there's no one to explain the rule you already
understood, no one narrating the obvious, and no social obligation to react
a certain way. You get to watch the actual game, at your own volume, on
your own couch, without performing your fandom for an audience.

## It changes how you watch

In a group, attention is split — half the room is watching the game, the
other half is watching each other's reactions to the game. Alone, you
notice things you'd otherwise miss: a defensive rotation, a coach's
tendency, the small adjustment at halftime that actually decided the
second half. It's closer to watching tape than watching a show.

## The counterargument, acknowledged

I'm not saying every game should be a solo affair. Finals, rivalry games,
anything with real stakes for a shared fanbase — those are better loud, in
a room, with people who feel it the way you do. That's a different kind of
good.

But the random Tuesday regular-season game that nobody else cares about?
That one's mine, and I've made peace with watching it exactly the way I
want to.
